\begin{abstract}
Self-evolving agents often store reusable ``skills,'' yet evaluations can conflate instruction text, supplied procedure output and executable procedure state. We separate these intervention surfaces while keeping the downstream benchmark request fixed. Persistent text shows little matched separation, and prospective procedure-output injection does not improve downstream findings. Executable state produces larger matched effects. On seven first-time STL-decomposition endpoints, the correct hidden implementation succeeds on 5/7 tasks while an identical-interface helper succeeds on 0/7, giving +71.4 percentage points with exact one-sided $p=0.03125$ under the frozen deterministic verifier. A deduplicated first-observation completion of the same pre-outcome candidate population gives 10/20 versus 2/20 ($p=0.00390625$) as robustness evidence without new closure authority. Measurement robustness remains open: the only valid arm-blinded semantic reviewer judges 6/7 targeted answers and 2/7 generic answers fully correct, giving $p=0.109375$; the second predeclared reviewer is nonvoting after a parse failure and is not replaced. We also test substantive task-local off-target procedures on previously unused L2 tasks spanning five non-STL families. Qwen2.5-32B gives 5/8 targeted successes versus 1/8 off-target (+50.0 points, $p=0.0625$), while DeepSeek-v4-Pro gives 5/9 versus 2/9 (+33.3 points, $p=0.125$). The non-STL directions are consistent across model families while both matched gates remain open. These results support matched executable interventions as a causal evaluation tool, while broader procedure-family generality, semantic measurement robustness and release-to-release persistent transfer remain open empirical questions.
\end{abstract}
