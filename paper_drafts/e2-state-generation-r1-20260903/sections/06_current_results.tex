\section{Current Results and Open Claim Gates}
\label{sec:results}

At this manuscript checkpoint, M0--M1 are complete and outcome-readable; M2 is an active frozen measurement object whose incomplete outcomes remain sealed; M3--M4 are conditional prospective stages. We report only completed evidence here.

\subsection{Rejected-witness evidence is not a stable universal treatment}
The 48-pair DeepSeek V2 study does not pass its frozen superiority criterion. The mean rejected-witness-minus-winner contrast is $+0.0231$, with bootstrap interval $[-0.0185,0.0660]$ and one-sided sign-flip $p=0.1719$. The stream distribution includes positive, null, and negative effects. This directly narrows the paper: the central contribution cannot be ``failed trajectories are better learning data.''

\subsection{A diagnostic-witness selector fails its development gate}
On the selected \texttt{e1-tsr-00} stream, First-Fail is 17/18 while WIN-C is 13/18, but progress-based failure and progress-contrast states are both 14/18. Because the prospective question was whether a more progress-diagnostic selector repairs the instability, the result is a failure of that hypothesis rather than evidence for First-Fail as a general rule. No S2 selector expansion is opened.

\subsection{State freezing and evidence replay point to different layers}
The historical strong First-Fail state remains directionally stronger than its WIN-C comparator when both states are frozen: 15/18 versus 14/18 in one complete remeasurement and 16/18 versus 12/18 in a second. The same pattern does not survive state regeneration from byte-identical First-Fail evidence. Fresh updater realization 1 yields 15/18 versus 15/18; realization 2 yields 11/18 versus 12/18.

Figure~\ref{fig:localization} summarizes the logic. The current evidence does not quantify a population variance component, but it separates two statements that were previously conflated: a persistent artifact can be behaviorally useful, while the updater that produced it can still fail to reproduce that artifact's utility from the same evidence.

\subsection{The strongest current claim}
The strongest manuscript-level claim supported today is deliberately local:

\begin{quote}
The usefulness of persistent skill self-evolution is not determined by trajectory evidence alone. In a controlled development case, a useful frozen state remains behaviorally active, but reconstructed byte-identical evidence does not reliably regenerate the same advantage through the native free-form updater. This localizes persistent-state generation as a candidate bottleneck that warrants an independent prospective intervention.
\end{quote}

The manuscript does not currently claim that the typed compiler improves downstream utility, that First-Fail-4 is superior to winner evidence, that the candidate bottleneck generalizes beyond the development substrate, or that the three repair primitives form a generally sufficient skill language.

\subsection{What changes after the next gates}
If M2 and M3 fail, the state-semantic sufficiency branch stops and the compiler experiment is not promoted. If they pass but M4 shows no compiler advantage, the state-generation-method story stops. If the compiler helps but First-Fail-4 does not, the paper pivots to reliable persistent-state compilation and drops failure-evidence superiority. Only a positive prospective generator effect plus the required falsifiers can justify changing the working title from ``Diagnosing State-Generation Variance'' to the method-positive ``Variance-Controlled State Compilation.''
