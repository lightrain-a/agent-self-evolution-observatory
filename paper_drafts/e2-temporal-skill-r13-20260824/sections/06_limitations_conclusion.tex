\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The unavailable TimeSage-EV evaluated snapshot prevents an exact rerun, so our 35-endpoint BEA/NOAA/EIA replay is independent mechanistic evidence with uneven resolution: EIA is cutoff-focused, same-domain grounding transfer has two held-out endpoints, Kimi alignment has three, and cross-domain grounding is null. DeepSeek is primary, Kimi a replication, and the mini model exploratory; repeats measure robustness rather than task sample size. G$_0$ establishes neutrality only under the frozen $\pm10$-point rule, and R rules out a material callable-container advantage only for the tested same-information integration surface. We therefore do not claim broad cross-domain/model-invariant transfer, neutrality of the historical G helper, or equivalence to every learned temporal retriever, indexing policy, or context budget.

\section{Conclusion}
Reusable temporal procedures can be binding repairs in specific non-ceiling model--task regimes. Requiring T to beat the original agent turns one apparent +28-point targeted-versus-generic cutoff effect into no repair; validating G$_0$ then separates operation value from generic-helper harm; and operation-matched R shows that the same repair need not live in a callable skill container. The strongest supported conclusion is therefore not ``skills help.'' It is that recurring temporal \emph{operations} can become causal bottlenecks, and those operations should be credited only after original-agent, neutral-compute, and same-information integration controls have been made explicit. In our tested regime, retrieval/context construction is as effective as callable skill state for carrying the operation.
