\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
We report endpoint-level contrasts and keep repeated executions inside each endpoint mean. The results are organized by what each added control changes about the scientific verdict rather than by a universal ``skills help'' narrative.

\subsection{The original-agent anchor overturns a two-arm gain}
\label{sec:falsepositive}
On DeepSeek C3-R cutoff, T succeeds on 100\% of endpoint-repeat observations and the complexity-matched target-blind stress helper G on 72\%. A conventional T-versus-G comparison therefore reports +28 points. N is also 100\%, however, so T--N=0 and the cell is \emph{no repair}. At endpoint means, T--G has 4 wins/1 tie/0 losses, whereas T--N has 0 wins/5 ties/0 losses, so the verdict reversal does not depend on treating repeats as independent tasks. A zero-call audit further confirms equal helper invocation and no detectable condition-position association; T prompts are 18.85 input tokens shorter on average. The point is not that G is a universally fair generic assistant, but that T-versus-stress-helper evidence can look convincingly positive while the original agent was never repaired.

\subsection{G$_0$ is a placebo diagnostic, not a universal neutrality assumption}
The independently frozen A2 study shows that G$_0$ is close to N when averaged over the full DeepSeek endpoint portfolio (G$_0$--N=$-2.1$ points, 90\% CI $[-9.3,5.0]$); Kimi is similarly close on its full endpoint set (+2.2 points, $[-4.3,8.7]$). These global summaries do \emph{not} transport into every small load-bearing stratum. On the exact A2 C3 and C4 grounding strata, G$_0$--N is +5 points and the 90\% intervals extend to +15 points; on the eight EIA held-out endpoints it is $-18.8$ points with a wide interval. We therefore remove stratum-level ``behaviorally neutral'' language and use G$_0$ as a same-surface no-operation placebo.

The three contrasts are more informative when kept separate. In a fresh two-repeat tranche, DeepSeek C3 grounding has T--N=+30, T--G$_0$=+30, and G$_0$--N=0 points (2 endpoint wins, 3 ties, 0 losses for the first two contrasts); C4 grounding gives +20, +20, and 0 (1 win, 4 ties, 0 losses). The endpoint-bootstrap intervals include zero because each cell has five endpoints. These are directional net-repair and same-surface output results with zero \emph{observed} placebo shift, not formal stratum-level equivalence or pure-operation identification.

The EIA cell provides a compact running example of the whole audit. On eight post-hoc compatibility-probe endpoints, fresh T--N is +37.5 points with 90\% bootstrap interval $[18.8,56.3]$, five wins, three ties, no losses, and one-sided exact sign test $p=0.03125$. T--G$_0$ is +62.5 points while G$_0$--N is $-25$ points, giving the exact contrast decomposition $+37.5=+62.5-25$. In the later exact-output surface check, T and R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ are both 100\% on these same eight endpoints. Thus this single example reads as: net repair over N, a larger same-surface contrast partly created by a harmful placebo shift, and no observed integration-surface residual at ceiling. We classify it as \emph{net repair with surface interaction}, not confirmatory cross-domain evidence or pure-operation attribution. The earlier five-repeat EIA execution is directionally consistent, but both executions reuse the same endpoint set and are not independent endpoint samples.

\subsection{Grounding survives only as a bounded DeepSeek result}
The historical DeepSeek grounding cells are 20/20/60 for N/G/T in C3 and 40/40/60 in C4. Fresh N/G$_0$/T execution preserves +30 and +20 point net-repair and same-surface output directions with zero observed G$_0$--N shift in those tranches. Kimi does not supply cross-model grounding confirmation: its fresh same-domain grounding check has T--G$_0=-25$ points and T--N=0, and cross-domain grounding is also null. We therefore drop the earlier cross-model grounding-transfer interpretation and retain only a bounded DeepSeek grounding result.

\subsection{EIA is a post-hoc compatibility probe}
The EIA expansion is constructed from 16 fixed weekly releases. Source indices 3--14 deterministically define 12 endpoints; releases before April 1 form four earlier diagnostics and the remaining eight form C4-R4. The endpoint set, time split, scorer, T1/G1 hashes, and randomized condition order are frozen before EIA outcomes. But EIA itself was chosen post-review because its dated releases fit the already frozen cutoff interface. We therefore use it only as a mechanism-compatibility illustration, not as confirmatory or generic cross-domain evidence. The mini model is already at N ceiling on the same eight endpoints, further emphasizing regime dependence.

\subsection{Exact-output control passes a low-sensitivity frozen-margin check}
R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ deliberately computes the exact T operation output and changes only how that output enters the harness. Across 18 pre-frozen DeepSeek endpoints, T--R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ is 0.0 points (1 win/16 ties/1 loss). The preregistered 90\% bootstrap interval is $[-5.6,5.6]$; a paired TOST at the prospectively frozen materiality margin of $\pm10$ points passes (both one-sided $p=0.0121$), with paired-t 95\% CI $[-8.5,8.5]$ and bootstrap 95\% sensitivity CI $[-8.3,8.3]$. The margin was fixed before R outcomes and was not derived from the observed T--R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ variance. We treat the result as a \emph{low-sensitivity frozen-margin check}, not positive evidence that the surfaces are generally interchangeable: an observed-variance calculation gives only about 53\% probability of declaring equivalence at $n=18$ if the true mean were zero, and the portfolio is ceiling-heavy.

The zero mean is not produced only by ceiling cells: C3 grounding is 60\%/60\% under T/R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$, and C4 is 70\%/70\%. However, the four strictly non-ceiling endpoints have a wide 90\% interval $[-25,25]$, so non-ceiling equivalence remains unresolved; EIA is ceiling at 100\%/100\%. All 18 endpoint residuals are listed in Appendix~\ref{tab:rsurf-residuals}. Only two endpoint means are non-ties, both in C4 grounding, and they have opposite signs: one is +50 points because only T repeat 0 selects the registered successful span pair, while the other is $-50$ points because only R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ repeat 0 does so. In both endpoints the frozen operation-output object is content-identical across T and R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$; the other three arm-repeat outcomes at that endpoint follow the failing selection. This pattern is consistent with low-frequency generation/selection variability under different placement surfaces, not a stable one-direction surface advantage. A three-endpoint Kimi alignment tie is descriptive only. Because R$_{\mathrm{surf}}$ reuses T's exact output under a forced one-answer harness, no multi-turn callable-state or independent temporal-retrieval equivalence is implied.

\subsection{Bounded mechanism update: serving projection does not reproducibly generate the beneficial updater state}
\label{sec:r17-bounded}
A later controlled child study asks a narrower learning-interface question: holding the realized candidate search pool fixed, does the evidence view supplied to a persistent updater matter? We compare a winner-only view (WIN-C) with a richer memory-relevant view (MRW) over 48 paired units arranged as 12 streams with four replicates per stream. The paired mean difference is MRW$-$WIN-C $=+0.023148$. Under the frozen analysis contract, neither superiority nor practical equivalence is established, so the global projection effect remains unresolved.

The child study nevertheless separates state stability from treatment reproducibility. One exact historical First-Fail updater state remains beneficial when evaluated again: fresh evaluation replicates change the held-out outcome by $+1/18$ and $+4/18$. However, when the same rendered First-Fail evidence is passed through independently generated updater states, the corresponding differences are $0/18$ and $-1/18$. The replication gate therefore fails. The observed positive state is a real frozen-state existence/stability witness, but First-Fail evidence did not reproducibly generate that state under the tested updater.

This result does not alter the R16 repair/attribution verdicts above and does not justify rescue by adding providers, models, or seeds. Its bounded mechanism lesson is that evidence serving and updater-state generation are distinct control surfaces: even if a particular learned state is stable once obtained, a serving projection deserves causal credit only when it reproducibly changes the distribution of generated updater states. In the present R17 object, updater-state generation is the identified precision/reproducibility bottleneck and standalone promotion remains unsupported.
