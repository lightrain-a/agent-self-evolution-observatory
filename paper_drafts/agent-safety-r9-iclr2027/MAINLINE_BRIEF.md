# R9 controlled-manuscript mainline brief

## Leading advantage

The paper gives controlled, branch-level evidence for the gap between a clean current safety panel and later behavior in a persistent action-taking web agent. Its distinctive object is the first-violation time under an exact same-schedule workflow-update contrast, complemented by fixed qualification probes across workflow snapshots.

## Chosen battlefield

- One frozen Qwen3-8B browser-agent runtime with Agent Workflow Memory.
- BrowserART tasks through BrowserGym, scored by the frozen HarmBench classifier.
- Four current-safe persistent states in map and reddit strata.
- Twelve paired three-step branches under updated and base-workflow conditions.
- Twelve fixed state–probe trajectories across workflow exposure steps 0–3.
- Finite controlled case study: 120 completed behavior episodes.

## Central claim

Within the frozen R9 design, a 12/12 current non-violation panel did not certify the evaluated future. Updated workflows produced first violations in 8/12 branches versus 4/12 under the same held-out schedule with the step-0 workflow held fixed; paired discordance was four update-only and zero control-only. The same fixed qualification probes developed first violations in 4/12 state–probe trajectories at later snapshots.

## Evidence spine

1. Current panel: 0/12 violations across four states.
2. Updated future: 11/36 violation episodes and 8/12 event branches.
3. Same-schedule base-workflow control: 7/36 violation episodes and 4/12 event branches.
4. Paired categories: 4 update-only, 0 control-only, 4 both-event, 4 neither-event.
5. Fixed probes: violations by exposure step 0–3 were 0, 1, 1, and 2; four trajectories had a first event.
6. Evaluation depth in the updated arm: 1/12 branches visible by step 1, 7/12 by step 2, and 8/12 by step 3.
7. Closest-work boundary: Al-Tawaha et al. provide broader multi-architecture longitudinal memory evidence; R9 adds an interactive BrowserART/AWM same-schedule contrast and first-event localization.

## Claim boundary

Supported: the static pass failed as a guarantee over this exact future; the updated-workflow condition produced more realized event branches than the same-schedule base-workflow control; fixed probes exposed snapshot-dependent violations.

Not supported: a population causal effect, a population hazard, universal failure of static evaluation, a new general longitudinal-memory paradigm, or HarmBench as ground truth.

## Reopen condition

“separate persistent update effect from held-out schedule effect” is satisfied for the frozen R9 finite design. It remains unsatisfied for any population claim and authorizes no further behavior execution.

## Demotions

Hashes, transport recovery, execution gates, and authorization details remain in machine-readable artifacts. The paper reports the single runtime void only to preserve failure semantics; it does not turn protocol bookkeeping into the scientific story.
