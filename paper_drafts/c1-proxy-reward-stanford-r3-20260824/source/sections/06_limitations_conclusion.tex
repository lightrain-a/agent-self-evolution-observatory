\section{Limitations}
The original six-source intervention has two failure-branch provider incompletions, so its 4/4 estimate remains conditional on paired completion. The disjoint breadth replication reduces the source-count concern---all 16 fresh pairs complete and diverge---but does not identify the two missing original memories. Evidence still comes from one released Shopping trajectory collection. GLM-5.3 reproduces upstream divergence on four sources but misses the downstream practical-effect floor; writer-invariant effects are not established.

Downstream evidence separates intervention from transport. The forced $4\times4$ matrix shows conditional sensitivity when memory is experimentally injected, but those four futures are not retrieval hits under the original bank. The 36-task experiment uses exact released retrieval identity and the native ReasoningBank memory-message format, yet still substitutes frozen browser-state packets for unavailable live endpoints. Its 34/36 zero branch contrasts comprise 18 joint floors and 16 joint ceilings, and the matched no-memory effect is only 0.04514 despite $p=0.00147$. We therefore do not introduce a denser endpoint after observing this saturation, nor claim live end-to-end or population transport.

Cross-policy transport is unresolved: DeepSeek-V4-Flash returns no assistant text on the first frozen unit at both the original 900-token cap and the sole preregistered 2,200-token repair, after which further cap escalation/model substitution is forbidden. This support stop is not a scientific null. More generally, the claims are local to the released ReasoningBank mechanism (task-description embeddings, top-$1$, threshold 0.3); learned retrieval, multi-memory composition, or reward-dependent bank membership define different substrates. Token Jaccard, operation slots, and the corruption-rate calculation remain bounded diagnostics rather than general semantic or variance laws.

\section{Conclusion}
Terminal feedback in reward-conditioned external memory is also a state-update input. Under byte-identical experiences and a fixed ReasoningBank construction policy, 20/20 complete paired sources produce different persistent memories, and a stronger reward-preserving wording control does not absorb the original effect. Forced downstream swaps show that such states can matter (mean $|\Delta|=0.15625$, $p=0.00074$), but exact retrieval audits prevent equating intervention sensitivity with realized exposure.

After expanding the bank to 20 sources, 125/172 held-out Shopping tasks clear the released retrieval threshold; nevertheless, a 36-task native-wrapper experiment yields only 0.02083 mean success/failure contrast and a matched no-memory arm only 0.04514 mean memory-presence effect, both below the frozen 0.15 practical floor. The scientific object is therefore a decomposition: reward errors robustly change what is written, while realized behavioral impact is separately gated by retrieval exposure, interface, endpoint headroom, and policy/support. Evaluator reliability is thus a persistent-state reliability requirement, but downstream risk must audit the transport path rather than infer impact from memory divergence alone.
