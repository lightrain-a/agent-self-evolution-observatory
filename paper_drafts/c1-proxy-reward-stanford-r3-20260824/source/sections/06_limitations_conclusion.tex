\section{Limitations}
The original six-source intervention has two failure-branch provider incompletions, so its 4/4 estimate is conditional on paired completion. The disjoint 16/16 breadth replication reduces the source-count concern but remains one released WebArena Shopping collection. GLM-5.3 reproduces upstream write divergence on four sources yet misses the downstream practical-effect floor, so writer-invariant terminal effects are not established.

Native experiments use exact released retrieval identity and the ReasoningBank wrapper, but fixed released browser-state packets rather than live endpoints. On the 36-task support, terminal S/F separation is 0.02083; no-memory, raw-trajectory, and outcome-blind structured-memory controls each leave only small reward-conditioned contrasts. The neutral-control result is statistically detectable ($p=0.0048$) but highly concentrated: task 229 contributes 61.5\% of absolute effect mass and 87.7\% of squared-effect mass, removing it lowers the mean to 0.01786, and only 2/11 selected sources contain a nonzero task. Fractional-reference scoring is post-hoc and cannot replace the binary gate.

The first-action probe localizes branch-specific attenuation on a fixed-state interface (TV 0.06944, $p=0.5801$, 0/36 modal S/F differences), but it is not a formal mediation analysis. Its larger memory-versus-no-memory signal (TV 0.17014; six modal shifts) is descriptive, not confirmatory. DeepSeek cross-policy transfer remains a provider-support stop rather than a null. Claims are local to the released top-$1$/0.3 retrieval substrate; learned retrieval, multi-memory composition, reward-dependent membership, longer-horizon live interaction, and population risk are different objects. Token Jaccard, operation slots, next-goal lexical distance, and corruption-rate calculations remain bounded diagnostics.

\section{Conclusion}
A terminal evaluator can be a state updater when its label selects how experience is committed to persistent memory. Under byte-identical experiences, reward-conditioned writing changes durable memory on 20/20 complete paired sources and survives a stronger same-mode wording reduction. Direct injection shows that these states can have conditional leverage (mean terminal $|\Delta|=0.15625$, $p=0.00074$).

The native path tells a different, complementary story. Exact retrieval can expose memories broadly (125/172 held-out Shopping hits after bank expansion), yet the success/failure distinction is weak at first action (TV 0.06944, $p=0.5801$) and terminal outcome ($|\Delta|=0.02083$, $p=0.4289$). Omission, raw experience, an outcome-blind structured rewrite, and a denser endpoint do not reveal a broad practically large hidden branch effect. In particular, the structured neutral control yields only 0.04514 reward-conditioned separation, below the 0.15 floor, with 30/36 tasks identical across all five representational arms.

The central lesson is therefore stage-resolved: \emph{reward errors change memory before they change policy}. Memory-text divergence establishes persistent-state corruption; it does not by itself establish realized behavioral transport. Evaluator reliability is consequently also a persistent-state reliability requirement, but downstream risk should be audited through write, exposure, policy uptake, and outcome rather than inferred from the stored text alone.
