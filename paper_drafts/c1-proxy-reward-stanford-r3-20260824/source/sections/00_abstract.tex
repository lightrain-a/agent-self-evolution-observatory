\begin{abstract}
Many external-memory agents use terminal feedback not only to score an episode but also to choose how its trajectory is written into persistent memory. We isolate this write-time channel in released ReasoningBank/WebArena Shopping trajectories by holding each source experience byte-identical and counterfactually flipping only the success/failure reflection branch. The original four complete pairs all diverge (mean token-set Jaccard distance 0.735), and a preregistered breadth replication adds 16/16 divergent pairs across 11 intent-template families, yielding 20 complete paired sources. On eight disjoint controls, reward-mode divergence exceeds stronger same-mode semantic-preserving rewording (0.700 vs. 0.595; exact $p=0.0078$). A forced-memory $4\times4$ terminal replication shows conditional sensitivity (mean $|\Delta|=0.15625$, $p=0.00074$), but exact released-retriever audits reveal an important transport boundary. The original four-memory bank retrieves on only 8/188 held-out Shopping tasks and on none of the four terminal-test futures. Expanding to 20 sources raises retrieval exposure to 125/172 held-out tasks and yields 36 deterministic-evaluable futures; nevertheless, 288 retrieval-matched success/failure rollouts produce mean $|\Delta|=0.02083$ ($p=0.4289$). A 144-call no-memory arm gives a memory-presence effect of 0.04514 ($p=0.00147$), below the frozen 0.15 practical floor. Thus reward errors robustly change what is written, while realized downstream impact is separately gated by retrieval exposure, interface, endpoint headroom, and policy.
\end{abstract}
