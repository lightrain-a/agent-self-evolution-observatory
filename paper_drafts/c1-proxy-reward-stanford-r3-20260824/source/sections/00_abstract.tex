\begin{abstract}
Many external-memory agents use terminal feedback not only to score an episode but also to choose how its trajectory is written into persistent memory. We isolate this write-time channel in released ReasoningBank/WebArena Shopping trajectories by holding each source experience byte-identical and counterfactually flipping only the success/failure reflection branch. The original four complete pairs all diverge, and a preregistered breadth replication adds 16/16 divergent pairs across 11 intent-template families, yielding 20 complete paired sources. On eight disjoint controls, reward-mode divergence exceeds stronger same-mode semantic-preserving rewording (0.700 vs. 0.595; exact $p=0.0078$). A forced-memory $4\times4$ terminal replication shows conditional sensitivity (mean $|\Delta|=0.15625$, $p=0.00074$), yet exact retriever replay shows that none of those four future tests retrieves its source memory under the original bank. After expanding to 20 sources, 125/172 Shopping tasks clear the threshold and 36 deterministic futures are frozen. On these, 288 retrieval-matched success/failure rollouts yield mean $|\Delta|=0.02083$ ($p=0.4289$). Two matched 144-call controls---no memory and the raw writer-input trajectory---both yield mean absolute effects of 0.04514 relative to the rewritten memories, below the frozen 0.15 practical floor ($p=0.00147$ and $0.00775$). Reward errors robustly change what is written, while realized impact is separately gated by retrieval, representation, endpoint headroom, and policy.
\end{abstract}
