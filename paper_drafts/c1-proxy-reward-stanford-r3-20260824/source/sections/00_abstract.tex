\begin{abstract}
Self-improving agents increasingly turn terminal reward into persistent natural-language memory, so an evaluator error can alter the state reused by later episodes. We isolate this channel in the released ReasoningBank implementation using WebArena Shopping trajectories. Holding each source trajectory byte-identical, flipping only the reward-conditioned reflection mode changes all four complete paired memories, with mean token-set Jaccard distance 0.735; two additional failure-arm writes terminate at the provider response-state layer and remain a selection limitation. On a disjoint eight-trajectory control, reward-mode content divergence (0.700) exceeds stronger semantic-preserving same-mode rewording (0.595), with paired excess 0.105 and exact $p=0.0078$. Reusing the paired states changes the next structured action in 7 of 12 aligned probes. A frozen $4\times4$ source-memory--future-task terminal replication completes 256 fresh rollouts and yields mean absolute success-rate difference 0.15625 ($p=0.00074$). Effects are heterogeneous: 8/16 cells are zero, and 84.1\% of centered signed-effect variation lies in source--future interaction. A fresh 32-call no-memory control further shows that omission matches both memory branches in eight cells, is closer to success-memory in six, and closer to failure-memory in two. Thus reward-conditioned state changes are cell-specific rather than uniformly beneficial or harmful. In this configuration, reward reliability is a state-consistency requirement when terminal outcomes determine persistent memory.
\end{abstract}
