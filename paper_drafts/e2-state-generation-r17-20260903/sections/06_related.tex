\section{Related Work and Novelty Boundary}

The closest feedback study is \emph{Rethinking Self-Evolving Agent Skills}, which varies normal, failure-only, and success-only feedback across models and benchmarks and finds sparse, heterogeneous improvement~\citep{liu2026rethinking}. It already establishes that failed trajectories can matter; our question begins where that result stops: under matched evidence, does the state-generation realization itself remain a material factor?

SkillRevise diagnoses execution-grounded defects, retrieves reusable repair principles, and applies anchored skill revisions~\citep{liu2026skillrevise}. Structured diagnosis and repair principles are therefore prior art, not our contribution. WikiSkill similarly separates raw traces, persistent accumulated knowledge, and executable skills~\citep{tang2026wikiskill}; we do not claim experience-to-persistent-knowledge compilation as new. MindMemOS is the first-party skill-evolution substrate used by our free-form baseline~\citep{liang2026mindmemos}. Other recent systems study behavioral validation, sustained evolution signals, and skill-library adaptation~\citep{he2026skillcommit,yan2026skillevo,han2026sisl}.

Our residual novelty is narrower: \emph{matched-evidence state-generation variance as a causal object}, followed by a prospective Evidence$\times$Generator factorization. The typed compiler is not novel merely because it is structured; it matters only if controlling the state-writing interface prospectively changes utility or reproducibility relative to the native updater.
